X's open-source algorithm, explained

Every post on X gets a score. Here's the math.

A model guesses what you'll do with each post. X weighs every guess and adds them up. Here's that scorer, built as a mixing desk.

One channel for each thing you might do

Like, reply, repost, share… and four you'd rather not: block, not interested, mute, report.

The fader is a prediction

The knob is how much X cares

Each action has a fixed weight. A reply: 5, or 20 if you follow each other.

reply ×5 → ×20

Multiply the two

Chance × weight is what this channel adds to the score.

3% × 20 = +0.60

Likes barely count

A like weighs just 0.5, so it adds 0.03: a twentieth of that reply.

6% × 0.5 = +0.03

Four channels subtract

Huge weights, because they're rare. Even a 0.2% chance of “Not interested” costs 0.095.

Score = the sum of chance × weight over every action. A negative sum is squeezed to between 0 and 0.001; a positive one scores at least 0.001. Then posts from accounts you don't follow are multiplied by 0.75, and each further post from the same author in one batch by 0.625, 0.4375… down to 0.25. Fourteen smaller signals (clicks, dwell time, photo and video opens) weigh 0.4 or less and are left off the desk.

Source: xai-org/x-algorithm, default weights in home-mixer/params/param.rs (synced 8 Oct 2026). X can run other values in experiments. The chances in the examples are illustrative.
